User Story Practice
===================

PNC Mobile Banking Application
------------------------------

### Background

PNC Bank is enhancing its mobile banking application to make it easier for customers to perform common banking activities without visiting a branch or using the PNC website.

The application will be available for both iOS and Android devices. The following requirements have been gathered from customers, bank employees, product managers, and other stakeholders. The development team will use these requirements to create and refine user stories for the product backlog.

### Application Requirements

1. Secure Mobile Sign-In

Customers need a secure way to sign in to the mobile application using their existing PNC online banking credentials. Customers with supported devices should also be able to use biometric authentication, such as Face ID, Touch ID, or fingerprint authentication, rather than entering their password every time they use the application.

2. Account Summary

After signing in, customers need to see a summary of their PNC accounts. The summary should include checking, savings, credit card, and other supported accounts and should display the current or available balance for each account. Customers should be able to select an account to see additional information.

3. Transaction History

Customers need to review recent activity for an account. Transaction information should include the transaction date, merchant or description, amount, and whether the transaction was a credit or debit. Customers should be able to search or filter their transaction history when looking for a particular transaction.

4. Transfer Money Between Accounts

Customers need to transfer money between eligible accounts. They should be able to select the account the money will come from, the account receiving the money, and the amount of the transfer. Before completing the transfer, the application should allow the customer to review the transaction and correct mistakes.

5. Mobile Check Deposit

Customers need to deposit eligible checks without visiting a branch or ATM. The application should guide the customer through photographing the front and back of the check, entering the check amount, and selecting the account receiving the deposit. The customer should be able to review the deposit before submitting it and receive confirmation after it has been accepted for processing.

6. Send Money to Another Person

Customers need a convenient way to send money to people they know and trust using Zelle. The customer should be able to identify a recipient using supported contact information, specify an amount, optionally include a note, and review the payment before sending it. The application should clearly identify the recipient before the customer authorizes the transaction.

7. Bill Payment

Customers need to pay companies and other billers from the mobile application. Customers should be able to select an existing payee, choose the account from which the payment will be made, enter an amount, and specify a payment date. Customers also need to see upcoming scheduled payments and their recent payment history.

8. Debit and Credit Card Controls

Customers who believe that a PNC debit or credit card has been misplaced should be able to temporarily lock the card from the mobile application so that it cannot be used for new transactions. If the customer later finds the card, the customer should be able to unlock it without contacting customer service. The application should clearly show whether a card is currently locked or unlocked.

9. Account and Security Alerts

Customers need to configure notifications about important account activity. Examples include a balance falling below a selected amount, a direct deposit arriving, certain card transactions occurring, or important security-related activity. Depending on the type of alert, customers may be able to choose among push notification, email, or text message delivery.

10. Spending and Budget Tracking

Customers want help understanding where their money is going. The application should categorize eligible transactions into groups such as restaurants, groceries, entertainment, transportation, and shopping. Customers should be able to review spending by category and establish budgets that help them monitor their spending.

11. Find a PNC Location

Customers sometimes need services that require an ATM or physical branch. The mobile application should help customers find nearby PNC ATMs and branches. Customers should be able to view basic location information and obtain directions. Customers may also want to search in another area rather than using their current location.

12. Get Help

Customers experiencing a problem should be able to obtain assistance from within the application. The application should provide access to commonly needed help information and appropriate methods for contacting PNC. When possible, customers should be directed to the type of assistance most appropriate for their issue.

### Student Exercise

For each requirement, identify the appropriate user or persona, the capability the user needs, and the business or customer value provided by that capability.

Convert the requirement into one or more user stories using this general format:

As a [type of user], I want [capability] so that [benefit/value].

Then determine whether the resulting story satisfies the INVEST characteristics:

Independent – Can the story be developed with minimal dependency on other stories?
Negotiable – Does the story describe the need without unnecessarily prescribing the implementation?
Valuable – Is the value to the customer or business apparent?
Estimable – Does the team understand the story well enough to estimate it?
Small – Can it reasonably be completed within a sprint?
Testable – Can the team determine objectively whether the story is complete?

For stories that appear too large, identify ways they could be split into smaller user stories.

Finally, write acceptance criteria for the stories that you believe are sufficiently refined to enter a sprint. Identify questions that would need to be answered by the Product Owner before writing acceptance criteria for the remaining stories.
